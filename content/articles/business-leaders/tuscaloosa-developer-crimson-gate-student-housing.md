Cranes have become a familiar sight around the edges of the University of Alabama campus in recent years, and the latest addition belongs to Crimson Gate Properties, a Tuscaloosa-based development firm breaking ground on its third student housing complex near campus. Founder Holt Ferguson said the project reflects his belief that demand for quality off-campus housing still outpaces available supply, despite years of steady construction in the area.

"Every time we finish a project, people ask if the market's saturated," Ferguson said. "And every time, the next building fills up faster than the last one did."

## Reading the Local Housing Market

Ferguson got his start in real estate managing a small number of rental properties near campus before shifting toward ground-up development roughly a decade ago. He said his approach has focused on identifying gaps between what existing student housing offers and what current students actually want, rather than simply replicating whatever competitors have already built.

"Housing that worked well for students eight years ago doesn't necessarily work for the students moving in today," Ferguson said. "Expectations around amenities, internet reliability, even how bedrooms and bathrooms are laid out relative to roommates, all of that shifts over time."

The new complex will include a mix of unit sizes designed to appeal to both underclassmen looking for their first off-campus experience and upperclassmen seeking more privacy, a segmentation strategy Ferguson said came directly from surveys and focus groups conducted with current students before design work began.

## Managing Risk in a Cyclical Market

Student housing development carries its own particular risks, tied closely to enrollment trends at the university and the broader competitive landscape of nearby properties. Ferguson said Crimson Gate Properties has tried to manage that risk by building in phases rather than committing to oversized projects all at once, allowing the company to adjust based on how quickly previous phases lease up.

"If we built everything we eventually plan to build in one shot and enrollment growth slowed down, we'd be sitting on a lot of empty units," Ferguson said. "Building in phases lets us read the market as we go instead of guessing years in advance."

The company has also diversified its holdings somewhat beyond pure student housing, adding a small number of mixed residential properties in other parts of Tuscaloosa, a hedge Ferguson said protects the business from being entirely dependent on the university's enrollment cycles.

## Competing in a Crowded Corridor

With several other developers active in the same general area near campus, Ferguson said differentiation has increasingly come down to management quality and responsiveness rather than just amenities, since most new construction now offers similar features like pools, fitness centers, and study lounges.

"At this point, almost everybody's building a pool and a study room," Ferguson said. "What actually keeps a building full year after year is whether residents feel like problems get fixed quickly and whether the property feels genuinely safe and well-run."

Ferguson said Crimson Gate Properties has invested more heavily in on-site management staff than some competitors, a decision he credits with strong renewal rates at the company's existing properties. He expects the new complex to lease primarily through word of mouth from current residents at the company's other buildings, a pattern he said has held for previous projects.

"We're not trying to be the flashiest building near campus," Ferguson said. "We're trying to be the one people recommend to their friends the following year."
