Rows of shipping containers stretch across the yard behind Gulf Crescent Logistics' new warehouse just north of downtown Mobile, a facility founder Desmond Okafor says he built specifically to capture business tied to growth at the nearby port. The building, roughly triple the size of the company's original site, opened its doors earlier this year and is already running near capacity.

"We could see the volume coming through the port increasing year over year, and we didn't want to be the bottleneck when customers needed goods moved inland quickly," Okafor said. "This warehouse gives us the space to stage freight properly instead of scrambling to find room."

## Betting on Port Growth

Okafor started Gulf Crescent Logistics more than a decade ago with a handful of trucks and a single small storage unit, hauling goods between the port and distribution centers across south Alabama. As container traffic through Mobile grew, he said, so did the opportunities for smaller regional carriers willing to specialize in the last leg of the supply chain rather than compete with national freight companies on long-haul routes.

"We never tried to be the biggest trucking company in the state," Okafor said. "We tried to be the most reliable one for the specific job of getting freight off the port and into warehouses and retail backrooms across this region."

The new facility includes expanded cold storage capacity, a feature Okafor said was added in direct response to requests from customers in the food distribution business who needed temperature-controlled space closer to the port than what had previously been available.

## Growing the Fleet and the Workforce

Alongside the warehouse expansion, Gulf Crescent Logistics has added trucks and drivers to its fleet, though Okafor said finding qualified commercial drivers remains one of the toughest parts of running the business. The company has partnered with a local trucking school to help interested candidates obtain their commercial licenses, in some cases covering training costs in exchange for a commitment to drive for the company afterward.

"Recruiting drivers today is nothing like it was when I started this company," Okafor said. "You have to be willing to invest in training people yourself if you want a fleet big enough to keep up with demand."

The company has also brought on additional dispatch and warehouse staff to manage the increased volume moving through the new facility, with Okafor noting that many of the new hires came from the surrounding Mobile neighborhoods rather than from outside the region.

## Positioning for What Comes Next

Okafor said he views the new warehouse as a long-term bet on Mobile's continued importance as a logistics hub for the Gulf Coast, rather than a reaction to any single contract or customer. He pointed to ongoing infrastructure investment around the port as a signal that freight volumes are likely to keep climbing over the coming years.

"You don't build a facility this size for one good year," he said. "You build it because you believe the next ten years look like the last five, maybe better."

Still, Okafor acknowledged the expansion carries risk, particularly given how sensitive freight volumes can be to broader economic conditions. He said the company has tried to diversify its customer base across retail, food distribution, and industrial supply clients so that no single sector's slowdown could seriously threaten the business.

"We've built this thing slowly and carefully for a reason," Okafor said. "I'd rather grow at a pace we can sustain than chase every opportunity that comes along and end up overextended."
