East Alabama Health has stepped up its preventive screening efforts across Opelika and Auburn, organizing community health events aimed at identifying conditions such as high blood pressure, diabetes, and elevated cholesterol before they progress into more serious complications requiring hospitalization.

The push reflects a broader strategy among Alabama hospital systems to shift more attention toward preventive medicine, a change health economists have long argued could reduce costly emergency care down the line if conditions are caught and managed earlier. For East Alabama Health, which serves a mix of urban and semi-rural communities in Lee County and surrounding areas, the screening events are also a way to reach residents who may not otherwise regularly see a primary care provider.

## Meeting People Where They Are

Rather than relying solely on patients to schedule appointments at a clinic, the health system has organized screening events at community locations including churches, community centers, and local employers, an approach intended to reduce the barriers that keep some residents from seeking preventive care. Barriers can include lack of transportation, inflexible work schedules, or simply not having an established relationship with a primary care doctor.

At these events, residents can typically receive blood pressure checks, blood glucose testing, and cholesterol screening, along with an opportunity to speak with a nurse or health educator about the results. Patients whose results suggest a possible chronic condition are referred to a primary care provider for follow-up, with health system staff helping connect uninsured or underinsured residents to lower-cost care options where available.

## Chronic Disease Trends in East Alabama

Health officials in the region have pointed to rising rates of conditions such as type 2 diabetes and hypertension as a driving concern behind the expanded screening push. These conditions often develop gradually and can go unnoticed for years without routine testing, by which point they may have already caused damage to the heart, kidneys, or blood vessels.

East Alabama Health administrators say the screening events are designed as an entry point rather than a complete solution, with the goal of identifying at-risk residents early enough that lifestyle changes or medication can meaningfully alter their health trajectory. Officials note that chronic disease management works best when it begins well before a patient experiences a serious event such as a heart attack or stroke.

## Building on Community Partnerships

The health system has worked with local governments and civic organizations across Opelika and Auburn to identify locations and populations that may benefit most from expanded screening access, including neighborhoods with historically lower rates of routine healthcare utilization. Officials say partnerships with employers have also proven valuable, since workplace screening events can reach adults who might not otherwise take time off during business hours to visit a clinic.

East Alabama Health has said it intends to expand the screening initiative further, with plans to add additional testing options, such as screening for kidney function, in future community events. Officials say they are also exploring ways to track long-term outcomes among residents who attend the events, including whether those identified as at-risk go on to establish ongoing care with a primary provider.

For a fast-growing area shaped by Auburn University and its surrounding economic growth, hospital leaders say keeping pace with preventive health needs is an ongoing priority, one they expect will require continued investment in reaching residents outside the traditional clinic setting.
