A community clinic offering free and reduced-cost care in Dothan has reported rising patient volume in recent months, a trend clinic leaders attribute to continued demand among uninsured residents across the Wiregrass region who otherwise struggle to access routine medical care.

The clinic, which relies heavily on volunteer physicians, nurses, and community donations, provides basic primary care services, chronic disease management, and referrals to specialists for patients who fall outside the reach of traditional insurance coverage. Clinic administrators say many patients are working adults whose jobs do not offer health insurance benefits and whose income falls into a gap that leaves them without an obvious pathway to affordable coverage.

## Serving a Coverage Gap

Alabama has not expanded Medicaid eligibility in the way that some other states have, a policy landscape that healthcare advocates say leaves a portion of low-income working adults without an affordable coverage option, since they earn too much to qualify for existing Medicaid categories but not enough to comfortably afford private insurance premiums. Free clinics like the one in Dothan have often stepped into that gap, providing a safety net for patients who would otherwise delay care indefinitely.

Clinic staff describe a patient population that includes people managing chronic conditions such as high blood pressure and diabetes, conditions that require ongoing medication and monitoring to prevent serious complications. Without a free or low-cost option, many of these patients would likely go without regular checkups, increasing the risk that a manageable condition progresses to a medical emergency requiring expensive hospital care.

## Stretching Limited Resources

The clinic operates primarily through volunteer medical staff who donate their time outside of regular working hours, along with financial support from local churches, civic organizations, and periodic fundraising events. Clinic leaders say that as demand has grown, the challenge of matching available volunteer hours and donated supplies to patient need has become more pressing.

To manage rising demand, the clinic has adjusted its scheduling to prioritize patients with the most urgent needs while working to expand its base of volunteer providers, including reaching out to retired physicians and nurses in the area who may have time to contribute periodically. Clinic administrators say recruiting specialists willing to see referred patients, even occasionally and at reduced or no cost, remains one of the more persistent challenges, since many specialty services are not available through the clinic's own staff.

## A Regional Safety Net

The clinic's service area extends beyond Dothan itself into surrounding Wiregrass communities, including patients traveling from smaller towns in the region who lack a comparable option closer to home. Clinic leaders say this regional draw has been part of what has driven rising patient volume, as word of the clinic's services has spread among community networks and through referrals from other local health and social service organizations, including Enterprise-area agencies.

Officials involved with the clinic describe its mission as filling an immediate and practical need, providing a bridge for patients who might otherwise forgo care entirely rather than face the cost of an uninsured visit to a hospital or private practice. They note that the clinic cannot fully replace the kind of comprehensive coverage that insurance provides, particularly for patients who need ongoing specialty care or expensive medications, but say it remains an important resource for a population that would otherwise have very few options.

Looking ahead, clinic leaders say they hope to expand operating hours further and continue building relationships with local healthcare systems to secure additional in-kind support, including donated lab testing and imaging services for patients who need diagnostic workups beyond what the clinic can provide on-site.
