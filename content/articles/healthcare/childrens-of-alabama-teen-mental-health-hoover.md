At a Children's of Alabama outpatient clinic in Hoover, a new adolescent mental health program has begun accepting referrals, part of a broader effort by the pediatric hospital system to address demand that has long outpaced the availability of youth counseling services in the Birmingham metro area.

The program pairs licensed therapists with a psychiatric consultation team, allowing teenagers referred by school counselors, pediatricians, or primary care providers to be seen for an initial evaluation more quickly than through traditional referral channels. Clinic staff say the model is designed specifically to catch adolescents in the gap between routine pediatric checkups and specialized psychiatric care, a gap that has widened in recent years as demand for youth mental health services has grown.

## Addressing a Persistent Access Gap

Families across the Birmingham suburbs have described long waits to secure appointments with child psychologists and psychiatrists, particularly outside the urban core. By locating the new program in Hoover rather than solely at the main Birmingham campus, Children's of Alabama is hoping to reduce the burden of travel for families in south Jefferson and Shelby counties.

Clinic administrators say the program's structure emphasizes early intervention, screening adolescents for anxiety, depression, and related concerns during what is often their first contact with a mental health professional. Teens who need more intensive treatment are connected with longer-term therapy or, when appropriate, psychiatric care, while those with milder concerns can often be managed through short-term counseling closer to home.

School counselors in the area have been identified as a key referral source, since many adolescents first show signs of struggling with their mental health in the classroom rather than during a medical appointment. Children's of Alabama has said it is working to strengthen communication channels with area school systems so students can be connected to care more efficiently.

## Staffing and Scaling Challenges

Like many pediatric mental health providers nationally, Children's of Alabama has faced the challenge of a limited pool of child and adolescent psychiatrists, a specialty that has long struggled with workforce shortages relative to demand. The hospital system has said it is recruiting additional clinicians for the Hoover site and evaluating whether a similar model could be extended to other outpatient locations in the region, including potential expansion toward Tuscaloosa or Huntsville depending on demand and staffing availability.

In the meantime, the hospital is leaning on a hybrid approach that blends in-person visits with telehealth counseling sessions for follow-up care, an option that has proven popular among families juggling school schedules and transportation logistics. Clinic staff have noted that virtual follow-ups tend to reduce missed appointments among adolescents who might otherwise skip a session due to a lack of transportation.

## A Broader Statewide Concern

Mental health advocates across Alabama have pointed to adolescent mental health as one of the more pressing public health issues facing the state, citing elevated rates of reported anxiety and depression among teenagers in surveys conducted through schools and community organizations in recent years. While Children's of Alabama's new Hoover program addresses only a portion of that broader need, hospital officials describe it as a meaningful step toward closing the access gap in one of the state's fastest-growing suburban areas.

The health system said it intends to track outcomes from the first year of the program, including how quickly referred teens are seen and how many continue with recommended follow-up care, information it plans to use to guide decisions about further expansion. For now, families in the Hoover and greater Birmingham area have a new front door into pediatric mental health services that did not exist before, one that clinic leaders hope will catch more adolescents before their struggles become a crisis.
