The Auburn farmers market has expanded its summer schedule this year, adding extra hours, new vendor slots, and a lineup of live music intended to draw bigger crowds during what organizers describe as the market's most important stretch of the year. The changes come as local growers report strong early season yields and increasing interest from shoppers looking to buy directly from area farms.

Market organizers say the expansion was driven largely by vendor demand. With more growers in the Auburn and Opelika area interested in participating than the market's previous layout could accommodate, organizers worked with the city to secure additional space and extend operating hours into the early evening on market days, making it easier for working residents to stop by after their jobs end.

## New Vendors Bring Variety

This season's market includes several new vendors offering products beyond the traditional produce stands, including a local honey producer, a small-batch salsa maker, and a baker specializing in sourdough bread made with locally milled flour. Organizers say diversifying the vendor mix has been a deliberate strategy to keep shoppers engaged throughout the summer rather than relying solely on seasonal produce availability.

Returning produce vendors report that this year's harvest has been strong for tomatoes, squash, and peppers, staples that make up the bulk of midsummer sales. Several farmers noted that consistent rainfall earlier in the growing season helped crops along, though the coming weeks of peak summer heat will test how well plantings hold up through the market's remaining months.

A market vendor coordinator said the goal has always been to make the market feel less like a once-a-week errand and more like a community gathering spot, which is part of why organizers pushed to add music and extended hours this year.

### Live Music and Family Activities

New to this year's lineup is a rotating schedule of local musicians performing during Saturday morning market hours, a change organizers say has noticeably increased the amount of time shoppers linger at the market rather than making a quick stop. Area teachers have also volunteered to run a small children's activity table on select Saturdays, offering simple craft projects using items donated by market vendors, such as dried flowers and surplus produce unsuitable for sale.

The market has also partnered with a local nutrition assistance program to accept benefits from low-income shoppers, part of a broader push to make fresh produce more accessible across income levels in the Auburn-Opelika area. Organizers say uptake of the program has grown steadily since it was introduced, though they continue working to raise awareness among eligible households.

## Looking Toward Fall

With the expanded summer season now in full swing, market organizers say they are already planning adjustments for the fall, including a potential pumpkin and gourd vendor day and continued live music programming as temperatures cool. They are also exploring whether demand justifies adding a second weekly market day, something several vendors have requested given how quickly popular items sell out during peak morning hours.

For now, organizers say the summer expansion has exceeded expectations, with vendor applications for next year's market already arriving well ahead of the usual sign-up period. Local growers say the increased visibility has translated into stronger sales, and shoppers appear to be responding to the more festive atmosphere the market has cultivated this season. City officials have taken notice as well, citing the market's growth as an example of the kind of community programming they hope to support further in future planning cycles.
