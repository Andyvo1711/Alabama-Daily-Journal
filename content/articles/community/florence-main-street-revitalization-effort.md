Florence's downtown corridor is showing signs of renewed vitality as a local Main Street revitalization program continues its multi-year push to fill vacant storefronts, restore historic building facades, and draw foot traffic back to a district that struggled for years against competition from suburban retail development. Program organizers say the recent wave of small business openings represents the most visible progress the effort has produced to date.

The Main Street program, which follows a national model focused on preserving historic downtown character while supporting economic development, has worked in Florence for several years building relationships with property owners, recruiting prospective business tenants, and securing small grants for facade improvements. Organizers describe the work as slow and incremental, but say the cumulative effect has begun to reshape how the downtown looks and functions.

## New Businesses Fill Long-Vacant Spaces

Several storefronts that sat empty for years have recently opened as small retail shops, a coffee roaster, and a locally owned bookstore, additions that organizers say have helped create more consistent foot traffic throughout the week rather than only during special downtown events. Property owners who had struggled to find tenants credit the program's facade grant assistance and business recruitment efforts with helping close the deal on several leases.

Program coordinators say attracting these businesses required more than just available space. Much of the work involved connecting prospective business owners with financing resources, walking them through historic building code requirements, and in some cases directly negotiating lease terms that made the initial investment more manageable for small, independently owned operations.

A downtown development coordinator noted that recruiting the right mix of businesses has been just as important as filling empty storefronts, since a downtown that offers a genuine reason to visit, whether for food, shopping, or entertainment, keeps people coming back rather than making a single stop.

### Facade Grants Restore Historic Character

The program's facade improvement grants have helped several building owners restore original architectural details that had been covered over by decades of modernization, including original transom windows and decorative cornices uncovered during recent renovation work. These improvements, while modest individually, have collectively changed the visual character of several blocks, giving the district a more cohesive historic appearance.

Organizers say the facade grants operate on a matching basis, requiring property owners to contribute their own funds alongside program support, a structure intended to ensure owners remain invested in maintaining the improvements over the long term rather than treating the grants as a one-time handout.

## Events and Ongoing Challenges

Beyond storefront recruitment, the Main Street program continues to organize recurring downtown events, including evening art walks and seasonal markets, aimed at introducing residents to businesses they might not otherwise discover. Organizers say these events have proven effective at converting first-time visitors into repeat customers for participating shops.

Challenges remain, however, including a handful of buildings still in need of significant structural repair before they can be safely leased, and ongoing questions about parking availability as foot traffic increases. Program leaders say they are working with city officials on both fronts, though solutions to the parking question in particular remain under discussion.

Looking ahead, organizers say they hope to build on recent momentum by recruiting additional business types currently missing from the downtown mix, including evening dining options that could extend the district's activity beyond typical daytime retail hours. For a downtown that spent years fighting a narrative of decline, the recent run of openings has given both organizers and longtime residents reason for cautious optimism about where the revitalization effort is headed next.
