Anniston veterans now have a new dedicated resource in the community following the opening of a support center designed to consolidate benefits counseling, job placement assistance, and peer support services under one roof. The center, organized through a partnership between local veterans organizations and community volunteers, aims to reduce the runaround many veterans face when trying to access scattered services across different agencies and offices.

Organizers say the idea for a centralized hub grew out of repeated feedback from local veterans who described difficulty navigating the maze of federal, state, and nonprofit resources available to them. Rather than veterans having to travel to multiple offices or make numerous phone calls to different agencies, the new center is intended to serve as a single starting point where staff can help direct visitors to the right resource for their specific needs.

## Benefits Counseling and Paperwork Help

A significant portion of the center's early activity has centered on benefits counseling, helping veterans navigate claims paperwork that many describe as confusing or overwhelming, particularly for older veterans less familiar with online application systems. Volunteer counselors, several of whom are veterans themselves, walk visitors through the documentation needed for disability claims, healthcare enrollment, and other benefit applications.

Staff say a common theme among visitors is simply not knowing where to start or which benefits they may even be eligible for. The center's counselors spend considerable time simply helping veterans understand what programs exist before moving into the actual application process, an early step that organizers say often gets overlooked in more bureaucratic settings.

A center volunteer coordinator explained that many veterans who come through the door have been eligible for benefits for years without realizing it, and that connecting them with what they have earned is one of the most immediately rewarding parts of the center's work.

### Job Placement and Peer Support

Beyond benefits counseling, the center has begun offering job placement assistance, connecting veterans with local employers who have expressed interest in hiring former service members. Staff help with resume translation, converting military job experience into language civilian employers readily understand, a step that many veterans say has historically been a barrier in their job searches.

The center also hosts weekly peer support gatherings, informal sessions where veterans can connect with others who understand shared experiences of military service and the transition to civilian life. Organizers emphasize that these sessions are not formal therapy but rather a space for camaraderie and mutual support, with referrals available for veterans who need more structured mental health services.

## Community Backing and Future Plans

Local businesses and civic organizations have contributed furniture, office equipment, and initial operating funds to get the center up and running, while several area churches have provided volunteer support for administrative tasks. City officials have also expressed support for the center's mission, noting that Anniston has historically had a significant veteran population given the area's military history.

Center organizers say they hope to expand services in the coming months, potentially adding family support programming for spouses and children of veterans, as well as expanded hours to accommodate veterans who work traditional daytime schedules. They are also seeking additional volunteer counselors trained in benefits navigation to keep pace with growing demand.

For now, staff say the response since opening has been steady, with word spreading primarily through local veterans organizations and community word of mouth. Organizers view the center as a long-term community investment, one they hope will grow into an even more comprehensive resource for veterans throughout Calhoun County in the years ahead.
