A coalition of Montgomery civic groups, churches, and local businesses has launched a new summer mentorship program aimed at giving middle and high school students structured exposure to careers, college planning, and everyday life skills. The program, which began enrolling participants earlier this spring, pairs teenagers with adult mentors who volunteer their time over the course of the summer.

Organizers say the idea grew out of conversations among community leaders who noticed a gap in structured summer programming once the school year ends. While Montgomery has several established recreational and academic summer camps, fewer options exist specifically for teenagers looking for mentorship and career-oriented guidance rather than traditional day camp activities.

## Matching Mentors With Mentees

The program's structure pairs small groups of three to four students with a single adult mentor, typically a local professional, college student, or tradesperson willing to commit to weekly meetings throughout the summer. Mentors lead sessions covering topics ranging from resume writing and interview skills to budgeting basics and navigating the college application process.

Coordinators say they intentionally recruited mentors from a range of fields, including healthcare, skilled trades, education, and small business ownership, so students can explore paths beyond the most commonly discussed career tracks. Several mentors work in construction and manufacturing, industries with strong local demand that organizers felt deserved more visibility among area youth.

A program coordinator explained that many students simply have not been exposed to the full range of career options available in the Montgomery area, and matching them with mentors in varied fields helps broaden their sense of what is possible after high school.

### Weekly Workshops and Field Visits

In addition to one-on-one mentoring sessions, the program includes weekly group workshops held at a community center on the city's east side. Recent sessions have covered financial literacy basics, public speaking, and time management, with plans for additional workshops on entrepreneurship and civic engagement later in the summer.

Field visits are also part of the curriculum, with students scheduled to tour a local hospital, a manufacturing facility, and a city government office over the coming weeks. Organizers say these visits give students a chance to see potential career paths in action rather than simply hearing about them secondhand.

## Community Support and Growth Plans

The mentorship program relies heavily on volunteer time and small grants from local civic organizations to cover workshop materials and transportation costs for field visits. Several area churches have also stepped in to provide meeting space and light refreshments during the weekly group sessions.

Parents of enrolled students have expressed appreciation for having a structured, supervised option for their teenagers during the summer months, particularly one that focuses on skill-building rather than simply providing childcare. Several parents noted that the mentorship model gives their children access to adult role models outside their immediate family and school circles.

Organizers say they hope to expand the program next summer, potentially adding more mentor-student pairs and additional workshop topics based on feedback collected from this year's cohort. They are also exploring partnerships with local businesses interested in offering short internships or job shadowing opportunities for participating students who show particular interest in a given field.

For now, program leaders say the initial response has exceeded expectations, with more students expressing interest than there were mentor slots available. Organizers are already compiling a waitlist for next year and actively recruiting additional mentors from the community to help meet the demand.
