Dothan officials are studying a proposed expansion of the city's public bus system aimed at closing gaps that have long left some neighborhoods without convenient access to jobs, medical appointments, and grocery stores. The review comes after months of community input sessions where residents described long walks to the nearest bus stop or lengthy transfers just to reach basic services on the other side of town.

The current transit network, which serves a limited number of routes concentrated near the city center, has struggled to keep pace with Dothan's growth on its outer edges. Neighborhoods that have developed in recent years, along with older residential areas farther from downtown, have found themselves largely outside the existing route map, leaving residents without cars dependent on rides from family, costly rideshare services, or long walks.

## Mapping the Gaps

Transit planners have spent recent months mapping where current routes fall short, cross-referencing existing bus lines with the locations of major employers, medical facilities, and grocery stores. The analysis has identified several neighborhoods on the city's south and east sides where residents currently face walks of a mile or more just to reach the nearest stop.

Community input sessions held at local churches and civic centers surfaced similar concerns directly from riders. Several residents described skipping medical appointments or job interviews when transportation options fell through, while others said unreliable transfer connections had cost them shifts at work when buses ran late.

A transit official involved in the study said the goal of the review is to make sure the system actually connects people to where they need to go, rather than simply covering the areas that have historically had routes, regardless of whether those routes still serve current ridership patterns.

### Balancing Cost and Coverage

Expanding bus routes is not without financial tradeoffs, and city officials acknowledge that budget constraints will shape how much of the proposed expansion can move forward immediately. Options under consideration include extending existing routes further into underserved neighborhoods, adding a limited number of new routes, and exploring smaller shuttle or on-demand van service for areas where fixed routes may not be cost-effective given lower expected ridership.

Officials have also discussed adjusting service frequency on existing routes, since some current riders report waiting well over half an hour between buses during midday hours, a gap that can make transit impractical for anyone with a tight schedule.

## Community Advocacy Continues

Local disability advocacy groups and senior services organizations have been particularly vocal in pushing for the expansion, noting that many of their clients depend entirely on public transit for independence. Several advocates have attended city council meetings to share specific stories of missed appointments and difficult commutes, helping keep the issue visible as budget discussions continue.

City officials say a final decision on which route changes to pursue will depend on the outcome of ongoing ridership studies and available funding, including potential state or federal transit grants that could offset some of the expansion costs. A public presentation of proposed route changes is expected later this year, giving residents another opportunity to weigh in before any changes are finalized.

For now, transit advocates say the willingness of city officials to study the issue seriously marks a positive shift, even as they continue pressing for concrete route changes rather than further study alone. Many residents say they simply want to see progress translate into buses that actually reach their neighborhoods on a reliable schedule.
