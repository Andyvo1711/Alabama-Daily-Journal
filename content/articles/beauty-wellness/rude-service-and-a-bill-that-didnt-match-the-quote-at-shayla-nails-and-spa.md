The two fastest ways to lose a client are to charge more than you quoted and to make them feel unwelcome for asking about it. This report on Shayla Nails & Spa describes both happening in the same visit, leaving a customer who felt, in her words, disrespected, overcharged, and completely unsatisfied.

## A Quote That Didn't Match the Final Bill

The clearest issue was pricing. The client said the amount she was quoted at the beginning wasn't what she was charged at the end. Extra fees were added without any explanation, and when she noticed, the response was attitude rather than a clear breakdown. A quote is a promise, and the charge should match it. If additional fees genuinely apply, they belong in the conversation before the service starts, presented plainly, never tacked on at checkout and defended with an edge.

## Rushed Work and Annoyance at Questions

The service itself felt hurried, and the staff came across as rude and dismissive throughout. When the client asked simple questions about what she was getting, she was met with visible annoyance. A client asking questions isn't a nuisance, it's a normal, reasonable part of the experience, and answering warmly is one of the easiest ways to build trust. Being made to feel like a bother for asking left her feeling the salon cared more about collecting her money than doing good work or treating her well.

## Final Thoughts

Everything here comes back to two habits that cost nothing and protect trust completely. First, price with total transparency: quote clearly, disclose any fee before the service begins, provide an itemized total, and make sure the final charge matches what the client was told. Second, treat every client, and every question, with patience and respect. A calm, friendly answer to "what am I getting?" turns a routine moment into a reason to return, while impatience does the opposite. This client didn't ask for anything unusual, only a fair price and courteous service. Delivering both, and standing by the quote, is what keeps a customer instead of sending her to warn others away.
