A client chose Vicky A Nail Spa because of its good reviews and because it is around the corner from her home. She came in for something simple, a gel color change on her hands and feet, and left feeling unhappy about how the visit went and how long the polish lasted. She noted that the nails looked good when she left, which is worth recognizing. The trouble began afterward, and her account shows how much of a gel service's quality is invisible on the day.

## A Visit That Felt Rushed

The client says she felt rushed and that the technician "did not let my nails sit under the light very long before she was asking for them again." Curing time is one of the most important parts of a gel service. Each layer of gel needs enough time under a lamp that works properly to harden fully, and a layer that is under-cured can look fine at first and then lift or flake within days. A salon can help by posting the curing time for each layer, timing it with a visible timer, and making sure the lamps are working well. When a client is asked to remove her hand early, it is a sign the schedule is driving the service. If the day is busy, being honest about timing is better than shortening the steps that clients cannot see.

## A Missed Nail and a Final Check

She also said she had to stop the technician twice because a nail had been forgotten. A client who has to point out a missed nail naturally begins to wonder what else might have been skipped. A simple end-of-service routine can prevent this: count the nails on each hand and foot, hold the hands under good light, and ask the client to look them over before moving on to the next step. It takes less than a minute and signals care. The client's decision to speak up twice is also a good reminder that clients notice details, and that checking in during the service helps catch small slips early.

## Flaking Within Days and the Question of Value

By her account, the polish started chipping the next day and was completely flaking off before a week had passed. She had paid $60 for a gel color change and compared it with a previous color from a different salon that lasted three weeks. Early chipping can come from several places, including nail prep, the thickness of each layer, curing, or how the nails are used at home, and each is something a salon can review. A simple promise, such as a free touch-up within a week or two if gel chips early, shows confidence and gives clients an easy path back to the salon before they decide not to return. When the price is meaningful to the client, a clear explanation of what the service includes also helps her judge the value.

## Final Thoughts

Three ideas stand out. First, protect the steps clients cannot see, especially curing time, by using timers and well-maintained lamps and by avoiding the urge to hurry on a busy day. Second, finish every service with a quick count and a visual check, so no nail is missed and the client leaves confident. Third, stand behind the work with a clear repair window, and make sure clients know about it at checkout. A client who arrives excited about a salon's reputation is ready to become a regular, and these habits help earn that second visit.
