A client and her mother booked pedicures together at S Nails Spa Alabaster, hoping for a relaxing time side by side. They came to the salon because of its strong reviews, and the visit did not match what they had read. Her account is a useful reminder that a salon's reputation raises expectations, and that the first half hour of a visit often sets the tone for everything after it.

## The Wait and the Words Used to Cover It

The client says they arrived about 10 minutes early and were seated on time, which is a good start. After that, she says, they waited around 30 minutes before anyone came over, and during that time they were repeatedly told they were "next." Being seated promptly shows a client she has been noticed, but sitting there with no update can feel worse than waiting in the lobby. When a delay is unavoidable, the most helpful thing a team member can do is give an honest estimate, even if it is longer than the client hoped, and then check back at regular intervals. A simple "We're running about 20 minutes behind, can I get you a drink while you wait?" is far better than repeating "next" with no timeline. Clients can usually handle a wait, but they struggle with not knowing how long it will be.

## Two Guests, One Appointment

The pedicure for the client began, but her mother was still waiting. By the time the client's toes were being polished, her mother had been sitting for over 45 minutes without help, and she left without having a pedicure at all. When two people book together, they expect to be served at the same time, or at least close together. A booking should note that it is for two guests and the schedule should reflect two technicians, so one person does not begin while the other is left idle. If staffing is short, saying so up front and offering a different time, or starting both clients together with a smaller service, protects the visit. When one guest begins and the other must wait, a manager or front desk team member can step in to apologize, give an update, and offer something to make up for the delay.

## A Service That Was Just Okay

The client said her own pedicure was "just okay," that the polish was not great, and that nothing about the service stood out. She also said no one came over to explain the delays and that the atmosphere felt a bit unfriendly, and she still paid full price. For a place with strong reviews, a service that feels average is a letdown. A warm greeting, a quick check on the finish of the polish before the client leaves, and a short word of thanks are small habits that lift an ordinary pedicure. A well-timed apology and a small gesture, such as a discount or a complimentary add-on after a long wait, can also change how a client feels at checkout.

## Final Thoughts

Three ideas stand out. First, be honest about wait times and check in often, so clients are never left guessing or hearing "next" with no end in sight. Second, handle group bookings as a team, with matching schedules and a plan so that nobody ends up waiting while a companion is served. Third, make the atmosphere welcoming from the moment guests walk in, and acknowledge a delay with a sincere apology and a fair gesture. A salon with strong reviews has already earned the first visit, and careful attention to these details helps earn the return visit as well.
