A new client at Nails Lash Studio recently shared her experience after getting tips on a Saturday. She describes herself as "very picky with my nails," which makes her a client who notices details and cares about the result. That is valuable feedback for any salon, since a careful eye is exactly the standard a team should want to meet. Her account covers the durability of the set, the finish, and the price.

## Tips That Didn't Hold

By her account, the tips were "popping off" by Sunday and Monday, only a day or two after the appointment. She also described them as uneven, with acrylic applied thick and in places it did not belong. Early lifting often points to something in the foundation of the set, such as nail preparation, how well the tip is fitted to the natural nail, the balance of liquid and powder, or how much product is placed near the cuticle and sidewalls. Thick application can also make nails feel heavy and look bulky. A quick review of these steps, along with a finishing check under good light before the client leaves the chair, helps catch both lifting risks and uneven shapes. For a picky client, inviting her to look at each nail before she goes gives her a chance to ask for adjustments right away.

## A Price That Felt Difficult to Understand

The client says her daughter received a basic manicure with no soaking and no hand massage, with only the nails polished, and that the charge was $94. She called this "insane." The review does not say what was included in the total, so it is hard to know how the amount was reached, but the reaction shows how a surprising total can overshadow everything else. A short conversation before the service, confirming what is included and giving the expected price, helps clients know what to expect. If a service is simpler than the usual version, it is helpful to say so and price it accordingly. At checkout, an itemized receipt and a calm walk through each line gives clients confidence in the final number.

## Returning for a Fix

After three trips in three days, the client says she believes the salon should refund her money so she can have someone else do her nails properly. Three trips in three days is a lot of effort for a customer, and it suggests the repairs did not resolve her concerns. A clear repair policy, such as a free fix within a set number of days, is valuable, and it works best when the same experienced technician reviews the problem and stays with it until the client is satisfied. When repeated visits still do not solve the problem, offering a partial refund or credit can protect the relationship and show fairness.

## Final Thoughts

Three ideas stand out. First, treat a picky client as an opportunity: slow down on preparation, fit, and product placement, and check each nail with her before she leaves. Second, explain prices before the work starts, including what a basic service does and does not include, and provide an itemized receipt. Third, make repairs easy and effective by assigning an experienced technician, and be ready to offer a fair refund or credit if repeated visits do not fix the issue. A client who feels heard, even after a rough start, may still choose to give a salon another chance.
