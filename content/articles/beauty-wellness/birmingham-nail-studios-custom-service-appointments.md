Nail salons in Birmingham are rethinking how they structure a visit. Where a standard manicure once ran a fixed thirty minutes, several shops around the city have begun offering extended appointment blocks that include a short consultation on nail health, product sensitivities, and long-term care rather than just polish selection.

At Magnolia & Lacquer, a nail studio in the Five Points South area, owner Renee Castellano said the shift reflects what clients have been asking for informally for years. "People aren't just coming in for color anymore," she said. "They want someone to actually look at their hands and tell them what's going on before we start."

## A Slower Appointment Model

The extended-format approach typically adds ten to fifteen minutes to a standard service, during which technicians assess cuticle condition, nail thickness, and any signs of irritation from previous gel or acrylic applications. Several Birmingham studios have introduced simple intake cards for new clients, asking about allergies, recent illness, and medications that might affect nail growth or sensitivity to chemicals used in polish and removal products.

Industry observers in the region note that this kind of front-loaded consultation is more common in markets with higher client turnover and more first-time visitors, which fits Birmingham's growing population of newcomers relocating for work in health care, banking, and technology. A longer intake process gives technicians a chance to build rapport with clients who may not have an established relationship with a specific salon.

## Sanitation and Tool Practices Under Scrutiny

The renewed emphasis on service quality has also brought more attention to tool sanitation practices. Several Birmingham salons have moved toward single-use files and buffers, discarding them after each client rather than sanitizing for reuse. Owners describe this as both a hygiene measure and a selling point, since clients increasingly ask about tool handling before booking.

State cosmetology regulations already require disinfection of multi-use tools between clients, but salon owners say visible practices — sealed tool packets, disposable liners in soaking bowls, and gloves worn during more invasive services like cuticle work — reassure clients who have become more attentive to hygiene standards in personal care settings generally.

## Pricing Reflects Longer Sessions

The extended appointment format has come with modest price adjustments at some studios, generally framed as reflecting the added time rather than a premium tier of service. Clients seeking a basic, quick polish change can typically still book a shorter slot, while those wanting the fuller consultation pay a bit more and commit to a longer chair time.

For technicians, the change has meant adjusting daily schedules to fit fewer appointments per day, a trade-off some say has been worth it for client retention. "We'd rather see the same person every three weeks than a different person once," Castellano said.

## What It Means for the Birmingham Market

The trend has not reached every corner of the city's nail industry, and budget-focused salons continue to serve a steady customer base looking for speed and low prices over consultation-heavy service. But the shift among mid-tier and upscale studios suggests a broader move toward treating nail care as an extension of overall skin and hand health rather than a purely cosmetic transaction.

Whether the model holds as Birmingham's beauty market continues to grow will likely depend on whether clients are willing to pay for the additional time, and whether smaller studios have the staffing flexibility to offer it without sacrificing volume. For now, several owners describe the approach as a differentiator in a crowded local market rather than an industry-wide standard.
