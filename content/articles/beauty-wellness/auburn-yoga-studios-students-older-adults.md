Auburn's identity as a university town shapes nearly every local business, and its yoga studios are no exception. Several studios in the city say they have deliberately built class schedules and formats to serve two distinct groups that rarely overlap: Auburn University students looking for stress relief between classes, and longtime residents, many older, seeking gentler, more restorative practice.

At Tiger Creek Yoga near downtown, studio owner Paige Sorrentino said the split became obvious once she started tracking attendance patterns by class time. "Our late afternoon classes are almost all students," she said. "Our mid-morning classes are almost all retirees and empty nesters. They're really two different businesses under one roof."

## Structuring a Schedule Around Two Audiences

To accommodate both groups, several Auburn studios now offer more explicitly labeled class types, distinguishing faster-paced vinyasa or power yoga sessions aimed at younger, more athletic students from slower, alignment-focused classes intended for older adults or those recovering from injury. This labeling helps new students self-select into a class appropriate for their fitness level and goals, reducing the awkwardness that can come from an unprepared student showing up to an advanced flow class.

Timing has become an important tool as well. Studios report that late afternoon and evening classes, scheduled around the end of the university's academic day, draw heavily from the student population, while classes offered between mid-morning and early afternoon on weekdays serve residents with more flexible daytime schedules, including retirees and remote workers.

## Addressing Different Physical Needs

Instructors say the physical demands of teaching to these different populations require distinct approaches. Classes aimed at students often incorporate more dynamic movement and can push toward more challenging poses, reflecting both the age and general fitness level of a college-aged clientele. Classes for older adults, by contrast, place more emphasis on joint mobility, balance work, and modifications using props like blocks and straps.

Several instructors in Auburn have pursued additional training specifically in yoga for older adults, including certifications focused on osteoporosis-safe movement and fall prevention, reflecting a broader recognition that standard yoga instruction does not always translate safely to older bodies without modification.

## Seasonal Fluctuations Tied to the Academic Calendar

Unlike many businesses that see relatively steady demand year-round, Auburn yoga studios contend with pronounced seasonal swings tied to the university calendar. Attendance from student clientele drops sharply during summer break and around final exam periods, when students either leave town or prioritize studying over class attendance. Studios that rely heavily on student membership have had to build financial models that account for these predictable dips, often by offering discounted summer passes to retain whatever student population remains in town for internships or summer coursework.

## A Model Particular to College Towns

The dual-audience approach is not unique to yoga; several other fitness and wellness businesses in Auburn describe similar strategies for balancing student and resident clientele. But yoga studio owners say the physical and pacing differences between the two groups make the balancing act particularly delicate, requiring more class variety than a single-format studio might need in a market without a major university population driving demand.
