Montgomery's humid climate has long shaped how residents think about hair care, but stylists across the city say the conversation with clients has become more specific in recent years, moving beyond generic anti-frizz advice toward tailored recommendations based on hair type, texture, and how a client actually spends their day outdoors.

At Capitol Row Hair Studio downtown, senior stylist Marcus Delaine said the biggest change has been in how clients ask questions. "It used to just be, 'what stops frizz,'" he said. "Now people want to know why their hair reacts the way it does, and what actually works for their specific texture instead of a generic product."

## Understanding Humidity's Effect on Hair Structure

Humidity causes hair strands to absorb moisture from the air, which can disrupt the bonds that hold a style in place, leading to frizz, loss of curl definition, or flattening depending on hair type. Stylists explain that this reaction varies significantly between fine, coarse, curly, and chemically treated hair, which is part of why blanket advice tends to fall short for a broad client base.

For clients with finer, straighter hair, the concern is often more about volume loss and limpness than frizz, while those with curlier or coarser textures more frequently deal with expansion and definition loss. Montgomery salons have increasingly split their humidity-focused product recommendations along these lines rather than offering a single "summer" product line to every client.

## Seasonal Adjustments to Coloring and Chemical Services

Color services have also seen adjustments tied to the climate. Several stylists note that porous, color-treated hair tends to absorb ambient moisture more readily, which can accelerate frizz and shorten the life of a blowout. Some Montgomery salons now recommend bonding or sealing treatments alongside color services specifically for clients who plan to spend significant time outdoors during summer months, framing it as a maintenance step rather than an upsell.

Chemical straightening and relaxer services, still requested by a portion of the local clientele, require particular care in humid conditions, since incomplete sealing of the hair cuticle after treatment can lead to faster reversion. Stylists say client education on aftercare — including how soon hair can be exposed to swimming pools, rain, or heavy sweat — has become a more consistent part of the service conversation.

## Styling Techniques Adapted to the Climate

Beyond products, some stylists have shifted toward styling techniques better suited to humid conditions, including looser, more textured styles that disguise frizz rather than styles that rely on sleek, flat finishes prone to visible humidity damage. Braided and twisted updos have grown in popularity for both everyday wear and event styling, partly because they hold up better through Montgomery's long warm season than more structured blowouts.

## A Practical, Ongoing Conversation

None of this represents a dramatic transformation of the hair care industry so much as a steady refinement of advice suited to the region's climate. Stylists describe the shift as less about new products and more about matching existing tools and techniques more precisely to individual hair types and lifestyles.

For clients, the upshot has been more individualized consultations at the chair, with stylists asking more questions about daily routines, outdoor exposure, and styling goals before recommending a product or technique, rather than defaulting to whatever is newest on the shelf.
