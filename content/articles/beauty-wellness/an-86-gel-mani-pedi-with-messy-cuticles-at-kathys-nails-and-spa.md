The difference between a good set and a disappointing one often comes down to the finishing details. This report on Kathy's Nails & Spa describes a gel manicure and pedicure where those details were missed, leaving a client who felt her $86 bought a result that looked less polished than what she came in with. The encouraging part is that everything she pointed to is about tidiness and care, which any technician can strengthen.

## Details That Were Overlooked

By the client's account, attention to detail was the main shortfall. She said gel ended up on her fingers and toes rather than staying neatly on the nail, and that her cuticles were left looking messy rather than clean. These are the small things that make a set look professional: keeping the gel off the skin, tidying the cuticle area, and wiping away any stray product before curing. When gel floods the sides or sits on the skin, it not only looks unfinished but can lift sooner, so clean application actually helps the set last. A careful eye on each nail, and a quick cleanup pass before the top coat, is what separates a neat, finished look from one that feels rushed.

## Paying $86 for Less Than She Came In With

The value stung because of the result. At $86 for gel on both hands and feet, the client reasonably expected a clean, polished finish, and instead felt that her nails honestly looked better before her visit. When a client leaves feeling a service set her back rather than improved things, the price becomes the sharpest part of the disappointment. Matching the finish to the price, with attention to detail that shows in every nail, is what makes a customer feel the visit was worth it and worth repeating.

## Final Thoughts

The reassuring part is that this is entirely about care and tidiness, both easy to build into every service. First, keep the application clean: gel should stay on the nail, never on the skin, and the cuticle area should be neat and well tended. Second, do a final detail pass before finishing, checking each nail and wiping away any stray product, so nothing looks rushed. Third, make sure the result matches the price, since a client paying $86 should leave with a clean, polished set she is proud of. A little extra attention to the finishing details would have turned this visit into exactly that, and given the client a reason to come back rather than a warning to share.
