A pedicure is a sequence of core steps, and each one takes skill and attention. When several are skipped or done poorly, the result isn't a faster pedicure, it's an incomplete one, and at a $70 price the gap is impossible to miss. This report on Venus Nails describes a service where the fundamentals simply weren't there.

## The Basics, Missing

By the client's account, the essentials were skipped one after another. The bottoms of her feet were still rough afterward because the callus work wasn't really done. The technician forgot to cut her toenails entirely, and the client had to remind her. And her cuticles weren't tended to at all. These aren't extras, they're the backbone of a pedicure, and leaving them out means the client paid for a service she didn't actually receive.

## Nails With No Consistent Shape

The finishing showed the same lack of care. The client noted that her nails had no consistent shape, some rounded, some square. Even shaping across all ten nails is a basic mark of skill, and a mismatched set is one of the first things both the client and anyone looking at the nails will notice. Combined with the skipped steps, it left her feeling the service was well below what $70 should buy.

## Final Thoughts

The encouraging part is that everything here is a trainable skill, not a fixed trait. A strong pedicure comes down to reliably doing the fundamentals: proper callus care so the feet feel smooth, cutting and cleaning the nails without needing a reminder, tending the cuticles, and shaping every nail to the same consistent style. A simple step-by-step checklist helps ensure nothing gets skipped on a busy day, and a quick final look at all ten toes catches any mismatch before the client leaves. Investing in that training, and matching the service to the price, is what turns a disappointing $70 into a pedicure worth coming back for. As it stood, the client left feeling the visit was far too expensive for what she received, and said she wouldn't recommend it.
