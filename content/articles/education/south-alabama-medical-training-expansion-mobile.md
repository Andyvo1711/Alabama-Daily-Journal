The University of South Alabama is broadening its network of clinical training sites across the Mobile region, part of an effort to expose medical students to a wider range of patient populations and practice settings before they graduate. The expansion builds on the university's existing relationship with USA Health, its affiliated hospital system, while adding new rotations at community clinics and regional facilities along the Gulf Coast.

Medical school administrators say the additional training sites reflect a broader shift in medical education toward exposing students to community-based and outpatient care earlier in their training, rather than concentrating clinical experience primarily within large teaching hospitals. Officials in the college of medicine noted in a statement that outcomes tend to improve when students see the full continuum of care a patient experiences, from a rural clinic visit to a specialist referral.

## Serving a Diverse Coastal Region

Mobile and the surrounding Gulf Coast counties present a distinct set of health challenges compared with more urban parts of Alabama, including higher rates of certain chronic conditions and a patient population that includes both longtime coastal residents and a growing number of newer arrivals to the region. University officials say training students across a wider variety of clinical settings better prepares them to serve that mix of communities after graduation.

The expanded partnerships include community health centers that serve lower-income patients who may otherwise face barriers to consistent care, as well as specialty clinics that give students exposure to fields they might not encounter as frequently in a general hospital rotation. Administrators say the goal is not simply to add training slots but to diversify the types of medical practice students experience firsthand.

### Building Long-Term Physician Retention

Like many parts of Alabama, the Mobile area has faced periodic concerns about physician retention, particularly in primary care and certain specialties. University officials have pointed to research suggesting that medical students who train extensively in a particular region are more likely to eventually practice there, making the expanded local partnerships a potential long-term recruitment tool for the area's health care system.

USA Health has framed the initiative as mutually beneficial: partner clinics gain additional support from supervised medical students, while the university strengthens its training pipeline and its relationships with community health providers who might later serve as employers for graduates completing residency training elsewhere and returning to the coast.

## Coordinating Logistics Across Multiple Sites

Expanding training across more locations introduces logistical complexity, including transportation for students, scheduling coordination with a larger number of supervising physicians, and ensuring consistent educational standards across sites that operate independently of the university's own hospital system. Medical school administrators say they have developed a structured curriculum framework to make sure students receive comparable educational experiences regardless of which clinical site they are assigned to.

The university has said the expansion will roll out gradually, with additional training sites added as partnerships are finalized and vetted for educational quality. Officials have not specified an exact number of new sites but described the effort as an ongoing priority rather than a one-time expansion tied to a single semester.

For the university's medical school, the initiative represents part of a broader effort to strengthen its reputation as a training ground well-suited to preparing physicians for practice in a coastal, economically diverse region — one that includes both the urban core of Mobile and more rural counties further inland along the Gulf Coast corridor.
