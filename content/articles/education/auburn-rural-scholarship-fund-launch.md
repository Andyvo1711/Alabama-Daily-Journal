Auburn University has introduced a new scholarship fund aimed specifically at students from rural counties across Alabama, a population that admissions officers say has historically been underrepresented relative to its share of the state's high school graduates. The fund will cover a portion of tuition and, in some cases, help offset housing and transportation costs for students traveling long distances to attend classes in Auburn.

University officials said the initiative grew out of internal data showing that students from rural school districts often face steeper barriers to enrollment, including longer commutes home, fewer Advanced Placement offerings in their high schools, and less exposure to college recruiting events compared with students in larger metro areas like Birmingham or Huntsville.

## Targeting a Persistent Gap

Admissions staff have said for years that geographic diversity within the student body lags behind Auburn's efforts on other diversity fronts. Rural counties in the Black Belt region and parts of southeast Alabama have sent proportionally fewer students to the university's main campus, even as Auburn has expanded recruiting trips to high schools in those areas.

The new scholarship fund is designed to be paired with existing need-based aid rather than replace it, meaning students from qualifying counties could potentially stack multiple forms of assistance. University financial aid staff say the goal is to reduce the net cost gap that often discourages rural families from applying in the first place, even when a student is academically qualified.

## How the Fund Will Operate

Eligibility will be based primarily on high school location, with priority given to counties the university has classified as having lower historical enrollment rates relative to graduating class sizes. Applicants will still need to meet Auburn's standard admissions requirements, and the scholarship will not function as a separate admissions pathway.

The fund is being financed through a combination of university reserves and gifts from alumni donors, several of whom grew up in rural parts of the state themselves. Development officers have said that appeals tied to geographic opportunity have resonated strongly with donors who attended Auburn decades ago from small towns and want to see that path remain open for current students.

### Broader Implications for Rural Recruitment

Education researchers in Alabama have long pointed to rural recruitment as one of the more difficult challenges facing the state's flagship universities. Distance from campus, weaker broadband access for completing online applications, and limited college counseling staff at smaller high schools all compound the difficulty of reaching qualified students before they decide not to apply at all.

Auburn's new fund is unlikely to solve those structural issues on its own, but university officials describe it as one piece of a larger recruitment strategy that also includes expanded high school visits and partnerships with community organizations in rural counties. The admissions office has said it plans to track enrollment figures from targeted counties over the coming years to evaluate whether the scholarship is achieving its intended effect.

For families in small towns across Alabama, the announcement offers a concrete, if modest, reduction in the financial calculus that often steers talented students toward community colleges or in-state options closer to home. Auburn has said additional details on the application process will be released ahead of the fall admissions cycle, giving prospective students time to factor the scholarship into their college planning.
