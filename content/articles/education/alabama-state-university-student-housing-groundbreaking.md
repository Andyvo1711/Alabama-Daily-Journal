Alabama State University has broken ground on a new residence hall in Montgomery, a project university leaders describe as central to their long-term strategy for retaining and recruiting students at the historically Black institution. The new facility is intended to replace some of the university's older dormitory buildings, several of which have required repeated maintenance in recent years.

Housing officials at the university have said the existing residence halls, some dating back multiple decades, no longer meet the expectations of incoming students who increasingly weigh campus amenities alongside academics when choosing where to enroll. The new building is expected to include suite-style rooms, updated common areas, and modernized study spaces intended to feel more comparable to newer housing options at competing institutions.

## Housing as a Recruitment Tool

Enrollment officers across Alabama's public universities have said housing quality has become a more visible factor in where students choose to attend college, particularly for students who could otherwise commute or choose a less expensive option closer to home. For a university like Alabama State, which draws students from across the Southeast in addition to central Alabama, competitive on-campus housing is viewed as a meaningful part of the recruitment pitch.

University administrators have also pointed to demand pressures on existing dormitories, noting that housing has at times filled close to capacity in recent years, particularly during the fall term when incoming freshman classes arrive alongside returning upperclassmen who choose to remain on campus.

### Financing the Project

The residence hall project is being financed through a mix of state support and university bonding, a common approach for public university construction projects in Alabama. University officials have declined to release a detailed cost breakdown but described the financing structure as consistent with previous capital projects on campus.

Construction crews are expected to work through multiple phases, with the university coordinating scheduling to minimize disruption to current students living in nearby buildings. Campus officials have said dust, noise, and temporary parking changes are anticipated during the build-out, and the university has committed to communicating regularly with students and staff about construction timelines.

## Part of a Larger Campus Investment Push

The new dormitory is one of several capital projects Alabama State has pursued in recent years as it works to modernize a campus that includes a mix of historic buildings and more recent construction. University leadership has framed the housing project alongside other investments in academic buildings and athletic facilities as part of a broader effort to strengthen the university's competitive position among Alabama's public institutions and other HBCUs regionally.

Students currently living in the dormitories slated for eventual replacement have been told that any relocation would be phased and planned well in advance, with the university aiming to avoid displacing students mid-semester. Officials in the university's facilities office said in a statement that resident input has been sought throughout the planning process to ensure the new building reflects what current students say they want from campus housing.

The university has not yet announced a firm completion date for the new residence hall, citing the multi-phase nature of the construction timeline, but administrators have said they hope to have at least a portion of the new housing available for assignment within the coming academic years. In the meantime, the university continues to manage existing housing stock, prioritizing maintenance on buildings expected to remain in use the longest.
