The University of North Alabama in Florence has launched a campuswide artificial intelligence curriculum initiative, embedding AI-related coursework into programs well beyond the computer science department, including business, education, and communications. University officials describe the effort as a recognition that AI literacy has become relevant to nearly every field graduates will enter, not just technical disciplines.

The initiative includes new elective courses focused specifically on practical AI applications, as well as revisions to existing courses that incorporate discussion of AI tools relevant to each discipline. Business students, for example, are being introduced to how AI-driven analytics tools are reshaping marketing and operations decisions, while education majors are examining both the opportunities and ethical questions raised by AI tools entering K-12 classrooms.

## A Cross-Disciplinary Approach

University administrators say the decision to spread AI coursework across multiple colleges, rather than concentrating it solely within computer science, reflects feedback from regional employers who have said they need graduates across all majors to understand how AI tools function and where their limitations lie. Faculty involved in designing the initiative have said the goal is not to turn every student into a programmer, but to ensure a baseline of literacy about how these tools work and how to use them responsibly.

Faculty committees spent months developing course materials appropriate for students without technical backgrounds, an effort university officials describe as more complex than simply teaching AI concepts to computer science majors who already have relevant foundational knowledge. The curriculum emphasizes practical, discipline-specific applications over abstract technical theory for non-technical majors.

### Addressing Academic Integrity Concerns

The rollout has also included a significant focus on academic integrity, as faculty across the university have grappled with how AI writing and research tools intersect with traditional assignments. Rather than banning AI tools outright, many instructors involved in the initiative have opted to teach students how to use them appropriately and disclose their use, treating AI literacy itself as a skill worth developing rather than a threat to be eliminated entirely.

University officials say this approach reflects a broader shift happening at colleges nationally, where blanket prohibitions on AI tools have increasingly given way to more nuanced policies that distinguish between appropriate and inappropriate use depending on the assignment and learning objective.

## Preparing Students for a Changing Job Market

UNA officials have framed the initiative as directly tied to workforce preparation, noting that employers across sectors, from regional manufacturers to healthcare providers in the Shoals area, have begun incorporating AI tools into daily operations. Students entering the job market without any familiarity with these tools, university leaders argue, risk being at a disadvantage regardless of their chosen field.

The university has also emphasized ethical considerations throughout the curriculum, including discussions of bias in AI systems, data privacy concerns, and the appropriate limits of automated decision-making in fields like hiring, lending, and healthcare. Faculty say these discussions are treated as core to AI literacy rather than an optional add-on.

Implementation is rolling out gradually across departments, with some colleges further along than others depending on faculty readiness and course scheduling constraints. University leadership has said it expects the initiative to continue evolving as AI technology itself changes, describing the curriculum as a living framework rather than a fixed program. Administrators have pointed to the initiative as part of a broader strategy to position UNA as responsive to changing workforce demands across north Alabama and beyond.
