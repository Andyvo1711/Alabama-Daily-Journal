Troy University has continued expanding its slate of online degree and certificate programs designed for working adults, an offering that administrators say has become an increasingly important part of the university's identity beyond its traditional campus in Pike County. The programs draw heavily from students in southeast Alabama, including a significant number balancing coursework with full-time jobs or active military service.

University officials say the appeal of the online format lies in its flexibility, allowing students who cannot relocate or step away from work to pursue degrees in fields such as business administration, criminal justice, and education without disrupting their existing responsibilities. Troy has long maintained ties to military communities given its proximity to Fort Novosel and other installations, and that connection has shaped much of how its online offerings are structured.

## Meeting Students Where They Are

Unlike traditional undergraduate programs built around a residential campus experience, Troy's online offerings are designed around asynchronous coursework that students can complete on their own schedules, an approach particularly suited to service members who may be deployed or relocated with little notice. University administrators say maintaining continuity of enrollment despite these disruptions has been a central design consideration for the programs.

The university has also expanded academic advising resources specifically for online students, recognizing that distance learners often need different forms of support than students living on campus, including more proactive check-ins to prevent the kind of academic drift that can occur when students lack regular in-person contact with faculty and peers.

### A Regional Workforce Development Tool

Beyond serving individual students, Troy's online programs have increasingly been framed by university leadership as a workforce development resource for southeast Alabama more broadly. Employers in the Dothan and Troy corridor have said access to a flexible degree-completion pathway helps them retain employees who might otherwise leave the workforce entirely to pursue a traditional in-person degree elsewhere.

University officials have pointed to certificate programs in particular as a growing area of interest, since shorter credentials allow working adults to gain specific skills or credentials without committing to a multi-year degree program. These offerings have expanded in fields tied to business operations, healthcare administration, and public safety leadership.

## Balancing Growth With Academic Quality

As enrollment in online programs has grown, Troy has faced the challenge common to many universities expanding distance education: ensuring that growth in student numbers does not come at the expense of instructional quality or meaningful faculty interaction. The university has said it has invested in training faculty specifically in online pedagogy, rather than simply having instructors record traditional lectures for remote viewing.

Troy officials have also emphasized accreditation and employer recognition as key priorities, noting that online degrees carry the same institutional accreditation as their on-campus counterparts and are designed to be viewed identically by employers and graduate programs.

Looking ahead, university leadership has said it expects continued demand for flexible degree options, particularly among adult learners in Alabama's rural and semi-rural counties who may not have easy access to a four-year university campus. Troy has framed its online expansion as a long-term strategic priority rather than a temporary response to any single trend in higher education, positioning the university as a resource for the state's working population well beyond its physical campus footprint.
